# PRAVAH — Research Papers

This document contains research studies reviewed during the development of PRAVAH.

---

## 1. Prediction of Flash Flood Susceptibility of Hilly Terrain Using Deep Neural Network: A Case Study of Vietnam

### Key Focus
The study investigates flash-flood susceptibility in hilly terrain using historical flood events and multiple geo-environmental factors.

### Important Factors
- Rainfall
- Elevation
- Slope
- Drainage
- Land use
- Environmental characteristics
- Historical flood information

### Relevance to PRAVAH
This supports the use of terrain, rainfall and other environmental factors rather than relying on rainfall alone.

### Limitation Relevant to PRAVAH
The study highlights challenges related to model calibration, spatial scale and local environmental conditions.

### PRAVAH Response
PRAVAH combines multi-source environmental data with terrain-aware assessment.

**Reference:**  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S1526149222002673

## 2. A Deep Learning-Based Probabilistic Approach to Flash Flood Warnings in Mountainous Catchments

### Key Focus
The study investigates probabilistic flash-flood warnings for mountainous catchments using deep learning.

### Important Finding
Probability-based warnings can provide more information than a simple flood/no-flood decision.

### Relevance to PRAVAH
This supports providing both flood risk and prediction probability.

### PRAVAH Response
PRAVAH aims to represent:

- Flood Risk
- Flood Probability
- Prediction Confidence

**Reference:**  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0022169425000150

---

## 3. Prediction of Flash Flood Peak Discharge in Hilly Areas with Ungauged Basins Based on Machine Learning

### Key Focus
The study investigates machine-learning approaches for flash-flood prediction in hilly areas where hydrological measurements may be limited.

### Important Challenge
Hilly and ungauged regions may not have complete measured data.

### Relevance to PRAVAH
PRAVAH should not depend completely on a single sensor or data source.

### PRAVAH Response
The system combines available rainfall, weather, terrain, satellite, historical and sensor information and considers data reliability.

**Reference:**  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1998956326004271

---

## 4. Assessing the Influence of Human Activities on Flash Flood Susceptibility in Mountainous Regions

### Key Focus
The study examines how factors such as land use, vegetation and built-up areas can influence flash-flood susceptibility.

### Relevance to PRAVAH
Flood risk can change as environmental and land-use conditions change.

### PRAVAH Response
PRAVAH is designed for continuously updated risk assessment rather than treating risk as permanently fixed.

**Reference:**  
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1470160X23015595

---

# Summary

The reviewed research shows that flash-flood assessment can benefit from:

- Multi-source environmental data
- Terrain information
- Machine learning
- Probabilistic prediction
- Historical data
- Continuous monitoring

These findings form the research basis for PRAVAH.
