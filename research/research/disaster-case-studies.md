# PRAVAH — Real-World Disaster Case Studies

This document summarizes major mountain-region flood events that demonstrate the need for timely prediction, impact assessment and emergency decision support.

---

## 1. Uttarakhand Floods — 2013

### Event
In June 2013, Uttarakhand experienced extreme rainfall that triggered widespread flash floods and landslides across mountainous areas.

### Major Impacts
- Loss of life
- Damage to roads and bridges
- Damage to buildings and infrastructure
- Disruption of transportation
- Severe impact on local communities

### Why This Event Matters to PRAVAH

The event demonstrates the rapid transition:

Extreme Rainfall
↓
Flash Floods & Landslides
↓
Infrastructure Damage
↓
Community Impact
↓
Emergency Response

A warning about extreme rainfall alone does not indicate which villages, roads or infrastructure are likely to face the greatest impact.

### PRAVAH Connection

PRAVAH aims to connect hazard prediction with:

- Impact assessment
- Vulnerability prioritization
- Evacuation planning
- Continuous risk updates

## 2. Chamoli Flash Flood — 2021

### Event
On 7 February 2021, a sudden flood event occurred in the Chamoli region of Uttarakhand, affecting the Rishiganga and Dhauliganga valleys.

### Major Impacts
- Destruction and damage to hydropower infrastructure
- Damage along river valleys
- Loss of roads and other infrastructure
- Severe impact on communities and workers

### Why This Event Matters to PRAVAH

The event demonstrates that mountain hazards can develop rapidly and produce downstream impacts.

The important question is therefore not only:

"Will flooding occur?"

but also:

"Which downstream areas and infrastructure may be affected?"

### PRAVAH Connection

This motivates:

Hazard Prediction
↓
Terrain-Aware Impact Assessment
↓
Infrastructure Vulnerability
↓
Priority Areas
↓
Emergency Action

## 3. South Lhonak Lake / Sikkim Flood — 2023

### Event
In October 2023, the South Lhonak Lake outburst in Sikkim resulted in rapid downstream flooding along the Teesta river system.

### Major Impacts
- Flooding downstream
- Damage to roads and bridges
- Damage to hydropower infrastructure
- Changes to river channels
- Impact on settlements and surrounding infrastructure

### Why This Event Matters to PRAVAH

The event demonstrates how a high-altitude hazard can create a cascading downstream impact.

Mountain Hazard
↓
Sudden Water Release
↓
Downstream Flooding
↓
Infrastructure Exposure
↓
Community Risk

### PRAVAH Connection

This supports the need for:

- Satellite and remote-sensing information
- Terrain analysis
- Downstream impact assessment
- Infrastructure vulnerability prioritization
- Continuously updated risk information

NRSC / ISRO — Sikkim Flood Assessment

# Overall Observation

These events demonstrate that mountain-region flood disasters can involve:

- Rapidly changing hazards
- Complex terrain
- Cascading impacts
- Infrastructure vulnerability
- Limited response time

Therefore, an effective system should move beyond hazard prediction and support:

Prediction → Impact Assessment → Prioritization → Action
